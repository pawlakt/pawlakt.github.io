I'm going to say something I'm honestly a bit ashamed of — not because it's mean, but because I really wish it wasn't true: most of the junior software engineer candidates I've talked to recently are simply unqualified.

We're hiring right now. We did our homework — prepared a proper job description, defined the role, put the offer out there. The response? Tons of applications. Seriously, the volume is impressive, and for a moment you might even think: wow, the talent pool is huge!
Then the meetings start. And that's where the whole picture falls apart.

## What Do I Even Mean by "Unqualified"?

Let me be precise here, because I don't mean "they can't write a for loop."
What I mean is that most of these candidates don't know the industry standards yet. And those standards are not some boring corporate checkbox — they're exactly what makes you flexible. Flexible enough to pick up a different task every day: different scope, different technology, different business problem. That's the reality of the job, at least in our world.

Instead, what I keep seeing in CVs and hearing in interviews is a very specific pattern. People treat "knowing the React SDK" as a skill. Or "foundations of Next". Sometimes even "I can quickly host an app somewhere" 😄
And look — all of that is fine. Genuinely. It all looks good on paper.
But it heavily depends on who you're looking for.

## Who We're Actually Looking For

We're a startup. And in a startup there's only one way to scale: you need people who can carry multiple tasks from completely different areas of the business. Today it's a backend fix, tomorrow it's a data question, sometimes it's even a client-facing task — leading an analysis meeting, asking the right questions, writing down what actually matters.

I like to call these people **orchestrators**. People who know the foundations, understand *why* things are built the way they are, and — most importantly — want to evolve together with the company. Not "React developers". Not "Next developers". Orchestrators.

And here's the uncomfortable stat: out of all the candidates I've had the opportunity to speak with, not a single one met even 50% of my expectations in terms of how I'd imagine our future collaboration.
Not one.
I know how this sounds — "old man yells at juniors" 😄 But I'm not yelling. I'm mostly confused.

## "The Junior Market Is Closed" — Is It Though?

Here's the funny part. I keep reading everywhere that the market for junior devs is closed. Dead. Over. AI took the jobs, thank you, goodbye.

I definitely don't think so.

Yes, the game has changed — it's now fully driven by AI. But that's exactly the point: the deck has been reshuffled, again. If you had a proper understanding of the business before — or at least some imagination about how businesses actually work — AI can turn you into a superhero. One person can now cover ground that used to require a small team. That's not a threat for a junior with the right mindset. That's the biggest career cheat code I've seen in my lifetime.

What worries me much more than the skill gaps is something else: I don't see any real enthusiasm. No spark. Nobody who shows up and says "I've been building things non-stop and I can't wait to build more." And honestly, that's the one thing that can't be taught.

So when I hear people complaining that the market is closed, I can't help but ask — is the market closed, or is it just moving at a pace you're not willing to keep up with?

The opportunities are there. The table is open. But you have to actually sit down and play.
